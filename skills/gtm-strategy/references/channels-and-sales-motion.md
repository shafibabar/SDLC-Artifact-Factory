# Channels, Sales Motion and the Buying Group — Reference

Self-contained. Read this when choosing the primary channel, filling the Sales and Distribution Model, or writing the buying group for a B2B purchase. Sources: Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* (economic buyer vs champion, one coherent channel); Brent Adamson, Matthew Dixon et al., *The Challenger Customer* (buying groups, Mobilizers, no decision).

---

## 1. Channel options

Rate each option for **this beachhead**; the table is product-neutral, so the fit column is filled per product.

| Channel | Works when | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Product-led (free trial, freemium) | The buyer can reach first value alone, quickly | Free users who never meet a buyer; freemium cost to serve (see `./pricing.md`) |
| Content / search | The buyer starts by researching a problem | Long lead time before signal |
| Community (developer, practitioner, professional) | The user or buyer belongs to an active community | Credibility is earned slowly and lost fast |
| Partner / referral (consultants, advisers, marketplaces) | A trusted party already advises the beachhead | The partner's incentives; trust transfer cuts both ways |
| Direct sales (outbound) | Contract value pays for a seller's time | High acquisition cost; only for accounts that justify it |
| Events | After early traction, to scale a working message | Expensive before the message is proven |

**The rule:** one primary channel that reaches the whole beachhead and is expected to carry 70%+ of early acquisition, plus at most two supporting channels. List deferred channels with when they will be revisited (usually after Stage 2). A channel the strategy kernel rules out is not an option.

## 2. Sales and distribution decisions

| Decision | Options |
|---|---|
| Sales motion | Self-serve (no seller) · product-assisted (a person helps a trial convert) · sales-led (a seller runs the deal) |
| Contract model | Monthly · annual · multi-year · consumption |
| Procurement path | Card · invoice / purchase order · master services agreement and security review |
| Trial model | Free trial (time-limited) · freemium (feature- or usage-limited; a monetisation model too, see `./pricing.md`) · proof of concept · demo only |
| Expansion motion | Follows the charging unit: a usage unit expands by usage, a seat unit by seats, a module offer by modules |
| Buying group | Who takes part, the Mobilizer target, and where consensus is built (in the trial, product-assisted, or a collective-learning session) |

There is no default motion: it follows from the beachhead's contract value, how fast the buyer can reach value alone, and how many people must agree. Each decision has one home: the trial model and expansion motion live here, but whether freemium is viable and which unit drives expansion are pricing decisions.

## 3. Economic buyer and champion (Moore)

Separate the **economic buyer** (owns the budget and the organisational risk) from the **user** and the **champion** (wants the product). A pragmatist economic buyer judges total cost of ownership, whole-product completeness and references, not technical elegance. A deal with only a champion has no one to sign.

## 4. The buying group (Challenger Customer)

B2B purchases are decided by a group spanning functions (finance, IT, security, legal, operations, the user team). The more people involved, the less likely the group is to buy at all. Deals rarely die because a competitor wins; they stall because the group cannot agree on the problem and drifts back to the status quo. **No decision is the main competitor** in a complex B2B sale, consistent with "doing nothing" in the competitive analysis's alternatives list.

**Profiles are attitudes, not titles.** *The Challenger Customer* sorts customer stakeholders into:
- **Mobilizers**, who can move the organisation: the Go-Getter (results), the Teacher (ideas, persuades colleagues), the Skeptic (tests everything; very influential once convinced).
- **Talkers**, who are easy to reach but cannot build consensus: the Guide (shares inside information), the Friend (likes you, takes meetings), the Climber (personal gain).
- **Blockers**, who defend the status quo. Do not invest in converting them; equip a Mobilizer to deal with them.

A CISO can be any of these. Name profiles as "likely" hypotheses per segment, to be confirmed in Stage 1.

**The test:** offer an insight (the Reframe). A Talker asks what it means for them; a Mobilizer asks what it means for the company and acts on it, or, as a Skeptic, pressure-tests it.

**One home: the stakeholder map.** The per-role table (role, must believe to say yes, likely objection, proof, likely profile, loses by staying put, confirmed by) is the buying-committee half of the stakeholder map (`stakeholder-mapping`), one per segment. The GTM cites it for the beachhead and never keeps a second table. If the beachhead is a segment the map does not cover, add its committee to the map first. Only when no stakeholder map exists (lean, where both artifacts are skipped) does the GTM write the hypotheses itself.

**What the GTM records:** the **Mobilizer hypothesis** (who will carry the change), the **Blocker** (who defends the status quo), and **where consensus is built**: get the group to agree on the problem before anyone compares solutions (collective learning); if members first meet over competing proposals, each defends their own priorities and the group stalls.

**Self-serve does not remove the group.** It appears at procurement, when security and finance review a trial a user started. At standard scale a group of two to four roles is typical; at enterprise, one per segment, with a consensus kit per role (see `./messaging-and-launch.md`).
