# Price Integrity, Discounts and Price Changes — Reference

Self-contained. Read this when writing the discount policy and approval ladder, answering a price objection, preparing the value brief for anyone who sells, planning a launch price, or changing a price for existing customers. Sources: Madhavan Ramanujam and Georg Tacke, *Monetizing Innovation* (value communication, discount discipline, post-launch feedback); Brent Adamson, Matthew Dixon et al., *The Challenger Customer* (buying groups, no decision). The give-get table and ladder shapes are this plugin's operational form of the book's discipline, not quotations.

---

## 1. Price integrity

**Price integrity** is keeping the realised price close to list. It erodes through ad-hoc discounts, unsustainable introductory prices and exceptions nobody records. Ramanujam and Tacke's rule: the price is defended by **value**, not by discount, and everyone who sells carries the value story.

Track: realised price vs list per tier, average and maximum discount depth, share of deals discounted, and the reason recorded for each.

## 2. Give-get

Every discount is traded for something the business values. No get, no discount.

| Get (what the customer gives) | Typical give (illustrative; set your own) |
|---|---|
| Longer term (annual, multi-year) | A term discount stated in the price book |
| Prepayment | A prepay discount |
| Volume above the tier's fence | Volume pricing already in the price book, not a one-off |
| A public case study or a reference call | A one-time discount or credit |
| A design-partner commitment (feedback sessions, early access) | A pilot price with an end date |
| Signature by a date the business genuinely needs (a launch reference) | Only with the reason written down |

**Not a get:** "to close this quarter", "they asked", "the competitor is cheaper" (answer with value first; see §4), "to be nice". End-of-period discounts teach buyers to wait for the period end.

## 3. The approval ladder (enterprise; a short list at standard)

| Discount depth | Who may grant | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Up to the price book's standard term / prepay discount | Anyone selling | The get is the term or prepayment |
| Above that, to a named limit | The account owner's manager or the product owner | A recorded get from §2 |
| Above the limit | The product owner (with finance, if one exists) | A recorded get and a business-case check |
| Any discount on a renewal | The account owner | It steps back toward list at the next renewal, with notice |

Name actual roles and numeric limits. "Sales may discount as needed" is a defect. A solo product owner is the whole ladder; the give-get still applies.

## 4. Price objections by buying-group role

In a B2B purchase the decision belongs to a buying group, each member weighing it against their own priorities (*The Challenger Customer*). A price objection from each role is a different objection:

| Role | What "too expensive" usually means | Answer with |
|---|---|---|
| Finance / economic buyer | Not budgeted; payback unclear | The value anchor as a payback line, in their numbers |
| Procurement | Above the last comparable quote | The difference in scope (the fence they are buying), a term give-get |
| The user team | Effort to switch outweighs the gain | Time to first value; what the switch costs |
| Security / IT | Hidden cost of a new data processor | The architecture or compliance pack (not a price cut) |

Give the Mobilizer this as part of the GTM's consensus kit. When the group has not agreed on the problem, "too expensive" is often **no decision** in disguise: the status quo costs nothing to defend. A discount does not create agreement; collective learning about the problem does. Record price-related losses in the competitive analysis's win/loss log and distinguish "lost on price" from "no decision".

## 5. The value brief

One page for anyone who sells or answers billing questions: the tiers and their fences in one sentence each, the value line per buying-group role, the give-get list, the ladder, and what never to promise (custom prices outside the ladder, roadmap features as a discount).

## 6. Launch and price-change rules

- **Introductory prices carry an end date** written in the price book and the customer's contract.
- **Existing customers at a price rise**: state whether they are grandfathered, for how long, and the notice period. Silence is a rule too: the worst one.
- **Moving a feature between tiers**: customers who bought for that feature keep it until their next renewal at least.
- **Pilot and design-partner prices** end on a date or a renewal, never "forever".
- **Review triggers** that reopen the price book: price-related losses in the win/loss log over a threshold; average discount depth above the ladder's standard; tier mix far from intent (for example, almost nobody on the middle tier); cost to serve moving by more than a stated share; a competitor price change recorded in the competitive analysis.
- After launch, win/loss outcomes and price objections feed back into the configuration and price (Ramanujam and Tacke). A price change that alters the GTM's range reopens the GTM first.
