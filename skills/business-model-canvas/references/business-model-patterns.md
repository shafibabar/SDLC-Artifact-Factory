# Business Model Patterns

Self-contained. Read this when the model gives something away free, serves two or more groups that need each other, sells many niche items, outsources a core layer, or licenses ideas or assets in or out. Source: Osterwalder and Pigneur, *Business Model Generation*, which names five recurring arrangements of the blocks; *Monetizing Innovation* (Ramanujam and Tacke) adds the freemium fence. Case companies in the books are illustrations, not evidence.

Naming a pattern tells you which blocks must interlock, and adds checks the generic coherence questions miss. Record the pattern (or "none") in the artifact, and run its checks as part of the coherence check.

| Pattern | What it is | Blocks it rearranges | Extra checks (all must pass) | Typical failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Free / freemium** | A substantial segment benefits from a free offer, paid for by another part of the model. Freemium: a free basic tier, a small paying share | Segments (free vs paying), Revenue, Cost | Cost to serve one free user, stated; expected free-to-paid conversion, stated as a hypothesis with a test; a **fence**: what the paid tier gives that the free tier never will; Fermi check that paying users cover all free users | The free tier is good enough, so nobody pays; free-user cost grows faster than conversion |
| **Bait and hook** (free sub-pattern) | A cheap or free first product, then recurring follow-up purchases | Revenue (recurring), Key Resources (lock-in) | What makes the follow-up purchase recurring and hard to source elsewhere | Customers buy the bait and source the hook elsewhere |
| **Advertising-funded** (free sub-pattern) | Users get it free; advertisers pay. A multi-sided platform | As multi-sided | As multi-sided | Users leave when the ads arrive |
| **Multi-sided platform** | Two or more distinct, interdependent groups; value comes from their interaction | Customer Segments (one per side), Value Propositions, Channels, Relationships and Revenue **per side**; Key Activity: platform management | A value proposition, channel and revenue entry for **each side**; which side is subsidised and why; the launch plan for the chicken-and-egg problem (which side comes first, and how) | Built for one side; the other never arrives |
| **Long tail** | Many niche items, each selling a little | Value Propositions (breadth), Key Resources (platform), Cost (low inventory) | The cost to add and hold one more item is near zero; how niche buyers find the item; often user- or partner-supplied content | Catalogue cost per item exceeds its sales |
| **Unbundling** | The firm separates its customer-relationship, product-innovation and infrastructure businesses (after Hagel and Singer), because each has different economics | All left-side blocks; Partnerships take over the unbundled layers | Which one of the three this firm actually is; who runs the other two, and the partnership type | Trying to win at all three with one culture and cost base |
| **Open** | Value created with outside partners: outside-in (bring in external ideas or IP) or inside-out (license out unused assets) | Key Partnerships, Key Resources, Revenue (licensing) | The partner and asset named; the revenue or cost effect of the exchange; who owns what is created | IP or dependency risk nobody priced |

**Segmented and diversified models** are not patterns in this list but need care: on a full canvas tag every value, channel, relationship and revenue entry with its segment, and give each segment its own cost-to-serve line where it differs. On a Lean Canvas, draw one canvas per segment.

**Choosing between patterns** (applying them to a specific product) is the agent's reasoning; these rows are the criteria it reasons from.
