# Value Proposition Canvas

Self-contained. Read this when writing the value block of a standard or enterprise canvas, when a segment has more than one buyer role, or when a reviewer asks how a value proposition was derived. Source: Osterwalder, Pigneur, Bernarda and Smith, *Value Proposition Design* (the companion to *Business Model Generation*). The typologies are recalled from the book; treat them as prompts, not quotations.

The Value Proposition Canvas zooms into two blocks of the business model canvas, Customer Segments and Value Propositions, and makes their **fit** visible. Write one per segment. In B2B, write one **Customer Profile per stakeholder role** whose pains differ (economic buyer, end user, influencer or recommender, and anyone who can block the sale), all against the same value map.

At **lean** no separate canvas is written: the Lean Canvas's Problem block holds the ranked pains and the UVP answers the top one.

---

## Customer Profile (the customer's side)

| Part | Types | Rank by |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer Jobs** | Functional (get a task done), social (how they are seen), emotional (how they feel); supporting jobs as buyer, co-creator or transferrer of value | Importance to the customer |
| **Pains** | Undesired outcomes and problems; obstacles (things that slow or block the job); risks (what could go wrong) | **Severity**, extreme → moderate |
| **Gains** | Required, expected, desired, unexpected | **Relevance**, essential → nice to have |

Write it from the customer's view, before the value map, and never from what the product does.

### Where the jobs come from

- **A `jtbd-analysis` artifact exists** (a revision, or Strategy re-entered after Ideate): cite its job statements and ranked desired outcomes by ID (opportunity is ranked on outcomes within a job, never on whole jobs). Do not rephrase, re-rank or add jobs here. If the canvas exposes a job the JTBD artifact lacks, record it as a hypothesis for `jtbd-analysis` to test, not as a new canvas job.
- **First Strategy pass** (`jtbd-analysis` is written later, in Ideate): take the main job from the vision's protagonist and the pains from the problem statement and competitive alternatives. Mark every job, pain and gain `hypothesis`. List them in the artifact's hand-off section so `jtbd-analysis` verifies and scores them; when it does, revise the profile to cite the result.
- The canvas never invents interview evidence. If no conversation has happened, the profile says so.

## Value Map (the offer's side)

| Part | What it lists |
|---|---|
| **Products and Services** | What the value proposition is built around |
| **Pain Relievers** | How the offer removes or reduces a **named, ranked pain** |
| **Gain Creators** | How the offer produces a **named, ranked gain** |

Draw a line from each reliever or creator to the pain or gain it addresses. In Markdown, a mapping table does this:

| Reliever / creator | Addresses | Rank |
|---|---|---|
| … | Pain P1 (severity: extreme) | 1 |

**Cut** any reliever or creator that points at no ranked pain or gain: it is a feature looking for a reason. A good value proposition addresses the few pains and gains that matter most, and does it very well; it does not address every one.

## Fit

Only the top-ranked pains and gains the map actually addresses go into the canvas's Value Propositions block. Three successive fits, each needing stronger evidence:

| Fit | Shown by | Evidence status it supports |
|---|---|---|
| **Problem/Solution Fit** (on paper) | Customers confirm they have these jobs, pains and gains, ranked as you ranked them | `tested` |
| **Product/Market Fit** (in the market) | Customers use, keep using and pay for the value proposition | `validated` |
| **Business Model Fit** (in the bank) | The value proposition sits inside a model that earns more than it costs, at scale | `validated` on the bottom rail |

## One-sentence check

Before writing the block, state the proposition in this shape (VPD's fill-in sentence, wording approximate): *Our [products and services] help [segment] who want to [job] by [reducing / avoiding a pain] and [creating a gain], unlike [competing alternative].* If a slot cannot be filled from the profile and map, the canvas is not ready. This overlaps `gtm-strategy`'s positioning statement: the canvas states the fit; GTM turns it into market-facing positioning.

## Generate before choosing

Write three or four candidate phrasings of the proposition before choosing one, and pick the one that names the top pain most specifically (Bier, *Unleash Your Creativity with AI*: the first candidates an AI produces are the most predictable). Record the rejected phrasings only if they were a different *proposition*, not a different wording.
