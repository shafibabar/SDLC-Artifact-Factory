# Example 2 — A B2B SaaS product: rewriting a positioning statement into a vision

Self-contained. The most common real-world case: a draft "vision" that is really a positioning statement, rewritten into a vision. The product is a data-estate intelligence platform for regulated SMBs.

## Input — the draft that was submitted as the vision

> For SMB compliance teams who cannot see their own regulated data, [Product] is a data estate intelligence platform that reveals every file, entity, and compliance gap within 30 minutes — running entirely inside the customer's own infrastructure, with no data ever leaving their boundary. Unlike cloud DLP suites that require data upload, it keeps data in place.

## Diagnosis

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Template | Moore's positioning statement: For… who… is a [category]… Unlike… (*Crossing the Chasm*) |
| Protagonist | The product is the grammatical subject; the compliance team is a qualifier |
| Horizon | "Within 30 minutes" is a present-day capability claim, not a future world |
| Category / competitor | "Data estate intelligence platform", "cloud DLP suites": tied to today's market (Dunford: category is a *choice* made in positioning) |
| Rules out | Nothing |

It is good positioning material. It goes to `gtm-strategy`, where the category and the alternative are supposed to live, and gets re-tested at each launch stage (Lauchengco, *Loved*).

## Working the components

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| Protagonist | Compliance and operations leads at regulated small and mid-sized organisations |
| Today's need | To *know*, and prove to an auditor, what sensitive data they hold and where it lives. (The want is "a scanner"; the need is confidence they can defend.) |
| Future state | They can answer that question on any day, not only after a weeks-long audit scramble |
| Why it matters | Smaller organisations shouldn't carry more compliance risk than enterprises just because they can't afford a team to look |
| Durable bet | Knowing your data must never require handing it to someone else |

## Output — the vision

> Compliance leads at regulated small and mid-sized organisations can say, on any day, exactly what sensitive data they hold and where it lives, and prove it, without handing that data to anyone. Knowing your own data stops being a privilege of enterprises with large teams.

**45 words.** No category and no competitor. The protagonist is the subject. It stays true whether the product ships connectors for Google Drive, S3 or something not yet invented.

**What this vision rules out**
- A hosted "upload your files" analysis tier for faster onboarding. It breaks the durable bet.
- Chasing large-enterprise DLP feature parity first. The protagonist is the team that *can't* afford that model.
- Remediation automation (deleting or moving data) as the core. The future state is *knowing and proving*; acting on the data is a different promise.

**North star implication:** *percentage of a customer's connected estate classified and audit-ready at any time*. It tracks "on any day", not "after setup".

**Where the 30-minute claim goes:** it is a real differentiator, so it goes in `gtm-strategy`'s positioning and messaging as a proof point. It doesn't belong in the vision.
