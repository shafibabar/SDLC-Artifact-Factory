# Example 1 — An internal tool (simplest case)

Self-contained. A small, single-office product with no market, no competitor and one user group. It shows that a vision still earns its keep at this size: it settles what the tool should *not* become.

## Input

> Problem statement: "A lunch-order tracker for a 20-person office. People pick from a daily menu by 10:30, an admin sends one combined order to the restaurant, and costs are split per person."

## Working the components

| Component | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Protagonist | The office's Order Admin, and their colleagues | The admin carries today's pain; colleagues share the future state |
| Today's need | Getting everyone fed on time without chasing people or money | The *want* is "a form"; the *need* is to stop being the office's unpaid chaser |
| Future state | Lunch arrives right and on time every day, and nobody owes anybody | Observable, and still true in 3 years |
| Why it matters | A daily chore stops eating one person's morning and goodwill | The stakes are small but real |
| Durable bet | The lightest possible habit wins: one choice, one cutoff, one exact split | Technology-agnostic |

## Output — the vision artifact (body)

> Every working day, everyone in the office gets the lunch they chose, on time, and nobody owes or chases anybody. The person who used to coordinate it gets their morning back, because ordering is one choice before a cutoff and paying is one exact split.

**45 words.** The protagonist is the subject and there is no category or competitor, so it passes "not a positioning statement".

**What this vision rules out**
- Becoming a catering marketplace that compares restaurants. That adds choices, while the bet is the *lightest* habit.
- Payments processing or company expense integration. "Nobody owes anybody" is met by an exact split, not by moving money.
- Multi-office rollout features before one office runs smoothly.

**North star implication:** *on-time, fully-settled order days per month*. A day counts only if the order arrived by the agreed time and every share is settled.

## What a weak first draft looked like

> "For a 20-person office who wastes time on lunch logistics, LunchTrack is a lightweight ordering tool that consolidates orders. Unlike group chats and spreadsheets, it splits costs automatically."

This is the positioning template. It names a category ("lightweight ordering tool") and competitors ("group chats and spreadsheets"), makes the product the subject, and rules nothing out. It is useful material for `gtm-strategy` (even an internal rollout has to win against the group chat), but it is not a vision.
