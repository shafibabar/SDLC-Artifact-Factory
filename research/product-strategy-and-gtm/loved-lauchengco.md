# Loved: How to Rethink Marketing for Tech Products
**Authors:** Martina Lauchengco
**Domain:** Product Marketing (within Product Strategy and Go-to-Market)

## Core Concepts

- **Product marketing is a distinct discipline that acts as the translation layer between product, go-to-market execution (sales, demand gen, partnerships), and customers — not a subset of either "marketing" or "product management."** Lauchengco's central claim is that product marketing translates product capability into customer-relevant value (outbound: what does this mean for the customer's problem) and translates market/customer signal back into product input (inbound: what is the market actually telling us). This repo has no artifact-producing role that performs the inbound direction at all: `skills/gtm-strategy/SKILL.md` is authored once during the Strategy phase and its Positioning Statement, Messaging Framework, and Launch Sequencing sections are never revisited by a defined mechanism once written — there is no channel through which post-launch market contact (a lost deal, a design partner's confusion, a support ticket that reveals the messaging didn't land) flows back into revising the positioning. The skill is currently a one-way, write-once artifact, not a living translation layer.
- **Five core functions of product marketing** — (1) deep customer/market/competitive insight, (2) positioning and messaging, (3) company and product narrative ("why this, why now"), (4) sales and channel enablement, and (5) demand-generation partnership. Mapped against this repo's existing skills: function (1) is `competitive-analysis`'s Steps 1–7 in substance; function (2) is `gtm-strategy`'s components 2–3 (Positioning Statement, Messaging Framework); function (4) is only half-covered — `gtm-strategy`'s "Sales and Distribution Model" component defines the *mechanics* of the sales motion (self-serve vs. sales-led, trial model, contract model) but produces no actual enablement *content* a design-partner conversation or trial UI would use; function (5) is only glancingly covered by `gtm-strategy`'s "Channel Strategy" component, which selects a channel but never establishes an ongoing briefing relationship with whatever runs campaigns on it. Function (3), the company/portfolio-level narrative, has no home in any of the four skills read here.
- **Positioning is a living hypothesis, continuously tested and refined against real market contact — not a document written once at launch and archived.** This is the book's sharpest departure from how this repo currently treats the artifact. `gtm-strategy`'s Quality Criteria table checks "Positioning honesty" (a named alternative plus a differentiator the alternative genuinely cannot match) at authoring time, but nothing in its Quality Criteria, Anti-Patterns, or the Launch Sequencing stage-exit criteria requires the positioning statement to be re-examined after Stage 1 (closed beta) or Stage 2 (soft launch) produces real evidence. `business-model-canvas`'s "Frozen canvas" anti-pattern is the closest existing analog — it requires re-validating the *whole canvas* at every launch-stage transition — but it is a general re-validation instruction, not a positioning-specific discipline tied to a named evidence source (win/loss outcomes) the way Lauchengco's treatment is.
- **Launch is a process spanning three distinct audiences — internal (the company must believe and consistently tell the story first), sales/partner (revenue-facing teams must be equipped to sell it), and external/customer (the market-facing announcement) — each with its own readiness gate, not a single announcement day.** `gtm-strategy` already has a strong, related anti-pattern — "Big-bang launch: skipping Stages 1 and 2 and going straight to GA" — but that anti-pattern is about *staged market validation risk* (don't scale acquisition before the ICP, messaging, and pricing are validated), a product-strategy concern. Lauchengco's version is a different failure mode entirely: a company can execute `gtm-strategy`'s three-stage sequencing (closed beta → soft launch → GA) correctly and still fail at each stage if the internal narrative is inconsistent across the team or the trial/sales flow was never actually equipped with the message. Both disciplines are needed and neither substitutes for the other; this repo currently has only the first.
- **"If it isn't written down, it doesn't exist" — but the book's version of this discipline goes one step further than documentation: the same written claims must show up verbatim (or near-verbatim) in every customer-facing surface, not just live in an internal strategy document.** `gtm-strategy`'s Positioning Statement section already enforces the documentation half of this ("Every claim in this statement must be defensible from the competitive analysis capability matrix") and its Messaging Framework's "Proof points must be factual and verifiable" is the same discipline applied to messaging. What's missing is the second half: nothing maps the approved Positioning Statement or Messaging Framework onto the actual trial-onboarding copy, README, or FAQ a real prospect will read — the message house is currently a Strategy-phase artifact with no defined path into the product's own customer-facing text.
- **The book's named organizational failure modes for how companies mistreat product marketing** — commonly summarized as reducing it to a "content factory" (collateral production with no strategic input), bringing it in only right before launch instead of during product development, conflating it with product management (confusing *what* gets built with *why/how it's talked about*), conflating it with demand generation (measuring it by MQLs/pipeline instead of message clarity and competitive win rate), and treating launch as a single event rather than a process. Several of these are **structurally moot in this repo's architecture**: `product-strategist` is the sole owner of both `competitive-analysis` and `gtm-strategy` from the Strategy phase forward per `CLAUDE.md`'s Component Frontmatter rules, so there is no separate PMM function that gets bolted on late or has its insight excluded from product decisions — one agent, one continuous involvement, by construction. The two failure modes that *do* have real, unaddressed surface here are the content-factory gap (enablement collateral is never actually produced, per the point above) and the demand-gen-partnership gap (no artifact establishes what a campaign owner needs from the positioning work).
- **A message house (headline claim, supporting pillars, proof points per pillar) is the book's structural unit for keeping messaging consistent across every surface.** `gtm-strategy`'s three-layer Messaging Framework (Headline → Value proposition → Proof points) is already a compact version of a message house and is a strong match — this is confirmed overlap, not a gap, and should be treated as validation of the existing skill rather than a place needing new content.
- **Win/loss analysis is a distinct discipline: capturing the actual outcome and stated reason of real competitive encounters (a trial that converted or didn't, a deal won or lost against a named alternative) and feeding it back into positioning.** `competitive-analysis`'s "Competitive Risks" table (Risk | Competitor | Probability | Impact | Mitigation, Step 7) is entirely forward-looking and anticipatory — it never captures what actually happened in a real evaluation. There is no mechanism anywhere in the four skills read to record "we lost this evaluation to a spreadsheet-and-consultant substitute because the differentiation point didn't land" and use that to revise the Positioning Statement or Differentiation Points — the empirical validation loop for positioning claims is entirely absent.
- **Company/category-level "why now" narrative — the macro urgency story (a regulatory shift, a market trend) that explains why the problem needs solving today — sits above any single product's positioning and is a distinct narrative layer.** `gtm-strategy`'s ICP table has a "Buying triggers" row ("events that make them ready to buy now") but this is scoped to a single prospect's readiness, not a company-level narrative used in the message house or an investor/partner conversation. Related but not the same artifact; worth naming as adjacent rather than conflating.
- **Regis McKenna / *Crossing the Chasm* lineage — Lauchengco explicitly credits Regis McKenna (with whom she worked early in her career) as a formative influence, placing this book in the same positioning lineage as Geoffrey Moore's work.** This matters directly for this cluster: the positioning-statement mechanics `gtm-strategy` already uses (target customer, category, differentiator versus a named alternative) trace to that same McKenna/Moore lineage. See Caveats for how this book's scope compares to Moore's (sibling file, not yet written) and to April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome* (sibling file, not yet written), which is also downstream of the same lineage but narrower in scope.

## Actionable Techniques

- Add a **Positioning Refresh Trigger** to `gtm-strategy`'s Anti-Patterns and Quality Criteria: the Positioning Statement and Messaging Framework must be re-examined at every Launch Sequencing stage transition (Stage 1 → 2, Stage 2 → 3) *and* whenever a real win/loss outcome contradicts a stated Differentiation Point — not left as a write-once artifact from initial Strategy-phase authoring.
- Add a lightweight **win/loss log** as an extension to `competitive-analysis`'s Step 7 (Competitive Risks) — for every completed trial or design-partner evaluation, record the outcome, the named alternative it was evaluated against, and whether the stated differentiator actually drove the decision. Feed this directly into the Positioning Refresh Trigger above.
- Before a Stage 1 → 2 transition (per `gtm-strategy`'s existing exit criteria: "ICP confirmed, core value delivered, onboarding completable without support"), add an internal-narrative readiness check: can the positioning statement's differentiator be explained, unprompted, in one paragraph, consistent with the written Messaging Framework? This is the internal-audience launch gate the book treats as separate from and prior to the external-audience gate `gtm-strategy` already has.
- Translate the approved Positioning Statement's proof points and the Messaging Framework's value proposition directly into the actual customer-facing surfaces (trial onboarding copy, product README, FAQ) as a required deliverable at the Stage 1 → 2 transition — close the gap between "message house exists as an internal document" and "message house is what a real prospect actually reads."
- Produce a minimal **sales/trial enablement artifact** — a one-page message house plus 3–5 objection-handling responses (e.g., "why not just use a spreadsheet," "why not BigID/Varonis") drawn directly from `competitive-analysis`'s capability matrix and Differentiation Points — so design-partner conversations and self-serve trial copy reuse the same claims the Positioning Statement already committed to, rather than improvising per conversation.
- Before proposing any new skill or agent from this book's content, apply a two-part test: does the gap describe an **organizational-ownership problem** (who is allowed to define positioning, when they're consulted) — largely pre-solved here by `product-strategist`'s single, continuous ownership — or a **content/artifact problem** (a work product that should exist but currently doesn't get produced)? Only the latter justifies new skill content in this solo-operator architecture.
- When drafting persona-specific messaging (see Applies To, `user-persona` row below), reuse each persona's existing "Information sources" and "Decision-making style" attributes to decide which proof points to lead with for that audience, rather than serving the buyer, user, and champion personas one undifferentiated Messaging Framework.

## Candidate New Skills/Agents

- **`sales-trial-enablement-content` (Skill, owned by `product-strategist`, consumed at the Stage 1 → 2 launch transition).** Teaches producing the concrete artifacts a real trial/design-partner conversation needs — a one-page message house, 3–5 objection-handling responses keyed to named competitive alternatives, and persona-tailored proof-point selection — derived from the already-approved Positioning Statement, Messaging Framework, and Competitive Analysis capability matrix. Justification: this is the one genuine content/artifact gap identified above that survives the organizational-ownership test — `gtm-strategy`'s "Sales and Distribution Model" names the sales motion's mechanics but has never produced enablement content itself, and no other skill fills this gap.
- **Not proposed as a standalone skill or agent: a dedicated "product marketing" domain/agent.** The book's chief contribution beyond positioning technique — the translation-layer thesis and its named organizational failure modes (late involvement, content-factory reduction, conflation with product management, conflation with demand generation) — describes problems that arise when product marketing is a separate function competing for seat and timing with product management and sales. In this repo's single-agent architecture, `product-strategist` already owns `competitive-analysis` and `gtm-strategy` continuously from the Strategy phase forward (per `CLAUDE.md`'s Component Frontmatter rules), so there is no separate function to be late, siloed, or reduced to content production in the first place — the organizational problem this book chiefly diagnoses does not exist here by construction. Standing up a distinct product-marketing agent would duplicate `product-strategist`'s role without a real organizational seam to justify it, against the frugality constraint.
- **Not proposed as a standalone skill: a full "positioning refresh" or "win/loss analysis" skill.** Both are genuine gaps (see Core Concepts and Applies To), but the fix is small enough to be an amendment rather than new skill surface — extend `gtm-strategy`'s Anti-Patterns/Quality Criteria with the Positioning Refresh Trigger, and extend `competitive-analysis`'s existing Step 7 table with a win/loss log column set, rather than authoring two new SKILL.md files for what amounts to a few new rows and a trigger condition on artifacts that already exist.

## Key Terminology

Candidates for `skills/glossary-management/references/ubiquitous-language.md` — none of these terms currently appear there (confirmed by search):

- **Product Marketing** — the discipline that translates product capability into customer-relevant value (outbound) and market/customer signal into product input (inbound); distinct from product management (which decides *what* gets built) and from demand generation (which drives campaign volume against an already-defined message).
- **Message House** — the structural unit for consistent messaging: one headline claim, supporting pillars, and proof points per pillar. `gtm-strategy`'s existing Messaging Framework (Headline → Value proposition → Proof points) is this repo's working implementation of the concept and should be cited, not redefined, once this term is added.
- **Win/Loss Analysis** — the discipline of capturing the actual outcome and stated reason of a real competitive encounter (a trial or deal, won or lost against a named alternative) and feeding it back into positioning — distinct from `competitive-analysis`'s existing "Competitive Risks," which is forward-looking/anticipatory rather than outcome-based.
- **Positioning Refresh Trigger** — a named condition (a launch-stage transition, or a win/loss outcome contradicting a stated differentiator) that requires an existing Positioning Statement to be re-examined, as distinct from a one-time authoring event.
- **Why Now (Category Narrative)** — the macro-level urgency story (a regulatory shift, a market trend) explaining why the target market's problem needs solving today; a company/category-level narrative layer distinct from a single ICP's per-prospect "buying trigger."
- **Sales/Trial Enablement Content** — the concrete, reusable artifacts (message house excerpt, objection-handling responses, persona-tailored proof points) that equip an actual sales, trial, or design-partner conversation — distinct from the internal strategy artifact (the Positioning Statement/Messaging Framework itself) that the enablement content is derived from.

## Caveats

- **A future reader should check the sibling files in this cluster (`research/product-strategy-and-gtm/crossing-the-chasm-moore.md`, `obviously-awesome-dunford.md`, `lean-product-playbook-olsen.md`) once all exist — this file was written without being able to read them, and overlap assessment here is necessarily one-directional.** In particular, cross-check every claim in this file's Core Concepts against Dunford's positioning content once that file exists, since Dunford's book and this one share the same positioning lineage (both descend from McKenna/Moore) and the risk of double-counting or contradictory framing between the two research files is real.
- **Expected overlap with April Dunford's *Obviously Awesome* is real but narrower than it first appears — be explicit about the difference, not just the similarity.** Dunford's book (based on general knowledge of its well-known structure, not read here) is a single-purpose, step-by-step positioning *methodology* — list true competitive alternatives, list unique attributes, map attributes to value, determine which customers care most about that value, layer in market category, check against relevant trends — usable by anyone in any role, independent of organizational context. Lauchengco's book treats positioning as **one of five functions** within a broader organizational discipline (product marketing) and spends comparatively little procedural depth on the step-by-step positioning method itself — `gtm-strategy`'s existing Positioning Statement template is already a compact version of what both books would refine, and Dunford's file, once written, should likely become the *primary* source for any future step-by-step positioning methodology content, not this one. What is genuinely distinct to Lauchengco and *not* expected in Dunford: the five-function definition of product marketing as an ongoing role, the launch-as-three-audience-process framework, sales/channel enablement as continuous work product, the demand-generation-partnership distinction, and the organizational "cardinal sins" catalog — none of these are positioning-methodology content, they are product-marketing-as-a-function content. A future reader merging both files into skill work should route positioning *technique* content to Dunford and positioning *lifecycle/organizational* content to this file.
- **Confidence level on the "5 Cardinal Sins" exact enumeration is moderate, not high.** This document summarizes the book's well-known, frequently-cited failure-mode catalog (content-factory reduction, late involvement, conflation with product management, conflation with demand generation, single-event launch thinking) from strong general/training-set familiarity with the book's public positioning and commentary, not from a fresh page-by-page re-read of the source text. The *substance* of each failure mode (and its mapping onto this repo's gaps) is used carefully and only where the underlying mechanism is well-established; the *exact wording and ordering* Lauchengco herself uses should be verified against the physical text before being quoted verbatim in any future skill's frontmatter or body.
- **The book's launch and enablement content assumes a company with separate product, marketing, and sales functions that must be kept in sync — this repo has one operator and one agent producing all Strategy-phase artifacts.** Every recommendation above that references "internal narrative alignment," "sales enablement," or "demand-gen partnership" must be scaled down accordingly: the internal-audience launch gate proposed in Actionable Techniques is a Shafi-reviewable checklist item, not a company-wide alignment exercise, and the sales-trial-enablement content is copy reused in a self-serve trial flow and design-partner conversations, not a distributed sales team's training material. Do not import the book's team-coordination framing wholesale.
- **This book is not itself a chasm-crossing or market-segmentation framework** (that is Moore's sibling file's territory) **and not a lean-experimentation framework** (that is Olsen's sibling file's territory) — its distinct contribution to this cluster is the ongoing-discipline and organizational-function view of marketing a tech product after the initial strategic bets (target market, category, positioning approach) are already made elsewhere. Treat it as the "how do we keep saying this consistently and correctly, forever" layer, not the "how do we decide what to say" layer.
