The response satisfies ALL of the following:
1. It rejects `CustomerUpdated` as the event name: a Domain Event names a specific business fact, in the past tense, in the domain's own language, and "updated" (a CRUD word for "the form was saved") says nothing a domain expert would recognise about what happened. It proposes one or more business-meaningful past-tense names for the address change, such as `CustomerRelocated`, `CustomerMoved`, `DeliveryAddressChanged` or `AddressCorrected` (introducing-eventstorming-brandolini, Core Concepts and Key Terminology: a Domain Event is "a fact in the past tense that matters to the business"; domain-driven-design-evans, Core Concepts: an event is "the record of something that happened"; wave-2-findings, domain-event-catalog § Eval ideas (a) and W2-39: no CRUD-only names).
2. It points out that "the address changed" can be different business facts, for example the customer moved house versus a typo in the address was corrected, which matter differently to consumers (a move may change the tax region and justify a local offer; a correction usually should not trigger a new-neighbourhood offer). It either asks the product owner which fact or facts happen, or models them as separate events, rather than collapsing them into one generic event (wave-2-findings, domain-event-catalog § Eval ideas (a): "asks which fact happened"; introducing-eventstorming-brandolini, Core Concepts: "A gap in the chain is a question to ask, not something to fill in silently", and Actionable Techniques: open questions go to the product owner).
3. It rejects `RecalculateTaxRegion` and `SendNewNeighbourhoodOffer` as things Customer Accounts publishes: these are Commands (instructions telling another context what to do), not events. Customer Accounts publishes only the fact, and each consuming context decides its own reaction as a policy it owns ("whenever a customer relocates, recalculate the tax region"; "whenever a customer moves to a new area, send a local offer") (introducing-eventstorming-brandolini, Core Concepts: the Process Modelling chain "command → system → event → policy → next command" and Key Terminology: Policy "whenever an event happens, issue a command"; microservices-patterns-richardson, Core Concepts: in choreography each service "subscribes to the others' events and reacts").
4. It rejects the `CustomerUpdated.v2` plan: a breaking change to a published event is released as a new event type with its own name, never a `.vN` suffix, and it defers the envelope and versioning rules to the event-schema-design skill rather than inventing them in the catalog (decision D047-1: "a breaking change is a new event type, never a `.vN` suffix; `event-schema-design` owns that rule and `domain-event-catalog` points to it"; wave-2-findings W2-3).
