# D031: Cite analytics-requirements' vanity-metric checklist as Croll & Yoskovitz's actionable-vs-vanity-metric framework (Lean Analytics, 2013) and add a clarifying distinction between the book's One Metric That Matters (OMTM -- stage-relative, deliberately rotating as the business's single biggest risk changes) and this repo's North Star Metric (okr-authoring -- fixed for the duration of an OKR cycle). Do not add OMTM as a new canonical glossary term at this time.

**Date:** 2026-07-24

**Rationale:** research/data-and-analytics/lean-analytics-croll-yoskovitz.md found the skill's existing five-point checklist independently reinvents most of the book's own framework without citing it, and separately found that OMTM and North Star Metric are superficially similar but genuinely distinct on one load-bearing dimension (durability vs. disposability) -- a reader who assumed they were the same term under different names would be wrong in a way that matters for how a requirement's priority argument should be read. The research file itself explicitly cautioned that adding OMTM to the canonical glossary is premature on the strength of one skill's cross-reference alone, since this repo has not adopted stage-based rotating focus as a standing practice -- that caution was followed rather than overridden.

**Alternatives rejected:**
- Add OMTM to the canonical glossary now, on the theory that any term explained in a skill body should also be glossaried -- rejected per the research file's own explicit caution: a term mentioned once for clarification purposes is not the same as a term this repo's artifacts actually use as a working concept, and premature glossary entries dilute the glossary's signal
- Treat OMTM and North Star Metric as interchangeable and merge the concepts rather than distinguishing them -- rejected: the two differ on a dimension (fixed-per-cycle vs. deliberately rotating) that changes how a requirement's priority argument should be read, so merging them would silently discard real information the research surfaced
- Propose a Lean Analytics stage-framework (Empathy/Stickiness/Virality/Revenue/Scale) skill or practice, since the book's OMTM concept is normally paired with it -- rejected as a poor fit for this repo's single, non-viral B2B product, per the research file's own Candidate New Skills/Agents section
